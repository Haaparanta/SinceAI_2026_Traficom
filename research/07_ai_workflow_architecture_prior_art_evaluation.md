# Prior art, architecture patterns, correspondence and evaluation for an auditable AI-assisted BR IFIC screening workflow

Scope note: this note covers tools and published AI work in the niche, auditable-AI architecture patterns, ingestion and RAG techniques, anomaly/trend detection on notice streams, the structure of inter-administration correspondence, and an evaluation strategy. ITU data formats, RR procedures, pfd mathematics, P.1546 and Finnish datasets are covered by other researchers and are only referenced where needed. Research date: 6 November 2026. Sources older than 2023 are marked "(older)". Items I could not open or verify are marked "(unverified)".

Method note on sources: many ITU documents are PDFs whose text is not exposed by HTML-converting fetchers; I downloaded them and extracted text locally with `pypdf` (which worked on Appendix 10, BR circular letters CR/269, CR/322, CR/526, and WRS-24 slide decks). Quotes below are from that extracted text unless stated otherwise.

## Key Question 1: Commercial and institutional tools, AI initiatives, and academic LLM work: what exists and what gap remains?

### Takeaway
Commercial suites (Transfinite Visualyse, LS telcom SPECTRA) and ITU's own BR software already do deterministic "check my networks against the BR IFIC" analysis and manage comment submission, but none found drafts expert-reviewable correspondence, ranks cases with evidence, or uses LLMs; published LLM work on the Radio Regulations is limited to RAG question-answering (KAUST 2025, CRC Canada 2024) with no filing-screening application, so "IFIC-to-draft-letter with provenance" is an open niche.

Academic and preprint work
- KAUST (El Kassimi, Fourati, Alouini), "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Reliable Interpretation of Radio Regulations" (arXiv 2509.09651, Sept 2025; AI4NextG @ NeurIPS 2025): corpus is the ITU Radio Regulations 2024 edition; retrieval uses all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings in FAISS; chunk sizes 150/300/500/700 words tested, "700-word chunks with top-7 retrieval performed best" reaching 97% retrieval accuracy (ROUGE-L F1 threshold 0.7); GPT-4o MCQ accuracy rose from 59.0% (baseline) to 70.9% with RAG, while inserting full documents without retrieval gave only 59.6%; DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B rose from 36.0% to 59.0% (60.0% with an LLM reranker at ~1.5x compute); the MCQ dataset was generated by flan-t5-xxl, filtered by a Llama-3-8B-Tele judge and a "light human pass", and "does not yet include a formal expert-verification stage"; the pipeline "has no explicit citation mechanism"; code and data: MIT-licensed repo — [arXiv 2509.09651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09651); [GitHub Zakaria010/Radio-RAG (MIT, 2 stars)](https://github.com/Zakaria010/Radio-RAG)
- Ghasemi and Guinand (Communications Research Centre Canada), "Accelerating Radio Spectrum Regulation Workflows with LLMs" (arXiv 2403.17819, March 2024) explores LLM roles in spectrum regulatory processes (stakeholder engagement, technical evaluations, licensing); the abstract reports no quantitative results — [arXiv 2403.17819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17819v1)
- Telecom-domain RAG prior art: Telco-RAG (arXiv 2404.15939) expands abbreviations with a 3GPP glossary and routes queries, reporting +14.45% on TeleQnA 3GPP questions vs GPT-3.5 without RAG; Telco-oRAG (arXiv 2505.11856) adds hybrid retrieval; TeleQnA (arXiv 2310.15051) is a ~10,000-question telecom MCQ benchmark — [Telco-RAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15939); [Telco-oRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11856v2); figures are from secondary summaries and should be checked against the papers
- "From Spectrum Regulation to Computational Enforcement: An Auditable Governance Architecture for Adaptive Spectrum Sharing" (SPECTRA-GOV, arXiv 2609.29571, submitted 30 Aug 2026) proposes a tri-layer architecture linking treaty coordination, national licensing and real-time enforcement with "causal provenance" so decisions can be traced; evaluation is simulated (10,000 scenarios per class; 0.028–1.682 ms enforcement latency); the authors state "these are software benchmarks, not field measurements" and no LLM is involved — [arXiv 2609.29571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29571)
- A 2025 survey, "Intelligent Spectrum Management in Satellite Communications" (arXiv 2509.00286), categorises AI/ML for dynamic spectrum management in SatCom; it does not address ITU filings, coordination procedures or LLMs — [arXiv 2509.00286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00286)
- A 2023 ITU workshop deck notes BR must "analyse, examine and translate for publication" large volumes of free-text "notes from administrations" in filings and that EPFD examination of very large constellations results in a "significant performance hit" — [ITU "Trends in non-planned satellite filings" (PDF)](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/study-groups/rsg4/Documents/Presentations/Workshop-ITU-in-Service-of-Space_Trends_v2.pdf)

### Inferences
- The competitive landscape is deterministic engineering software (Visualyse, SPECTRAemc, ITU GIBC/SpaceCom) plus web dashboards (SpaceExplorer). None of the sources describe ranking cases for a specific administration with attached evidence, generating draft comments/letters, or logging an audit trail of the review. That is the gap the hackathon solution should claim.
- Vendors integrate the IFIC .mdb directly; the ITU itself distributes comment tooling (SpaceCom) and now routes correspondence through e-Communications and HITS. A hackathon tool is therefore most useful as a pre-processor that produces (a) a ranked, evidenced case list and (b) content ready to paste into SpaceCom/e-Communications/HITS, not as a replacement filing system.
- The published LLM work shows that (i) retrieval over the RR beats long-context stuffing, (ii) chunk granularity matters a lot, and (iii) nobody has yet evaluated citation faithfulness on RR answers; a tool that cites RR provision numbers and evidence IDs would be ahead of the literature.

- ATDI (HTZ/ICS telecom) ITU-filing features: no results surfaced in searches; not verified.
- "Savvy" (named in the brief) could not be identified as a product; not searched further.
- No CEPT/ECC report on AI in spectrum management was found (two searches); only a LinkedIn post about a CEPT SG3 workshop that touched on AI (unverified).
- Ofcom's commenting-system page returned HTTP 403; details are from a search summary only.
- The KAUST dataset size (final N) was not stated in the fetched text.

## Key Question 2: Open-source tools and parsers (and their licences)

### Takeaway
No open-source parser specific to ITU SNS/SRS/BR IFIC databases or the Space Network List was found on GitHub, so the team should build a thin .mdb-to-SQLite snapshot layer on mdbtools (GPL-2.0) or the pure-Python access_parser (Apache-2.0); the propagation, geospatial and orbit stack is well covered by permissively licensed packages, with the important caveat that pycraf's ITU data files are not free for commercial use and eeveetza's reference implementations carry no SPDX licence.

### Cited Findings
- Searches for GitHub parsers of ITU SNS/SRS/BR IFIC and SNL scrapers returned only ITU's own Windows tools (SpaceQry, SRSConvert) and the SNL Excel reports; no community repository was found — [ITU software page](https://itu.int/ITU-R/go/space-software/en); [ITU SNL reports 2024 (docx)](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/snl/SNLReport/SNS-ref-list-2024_e.docx)

### Inferences
- For a 48-hour build, convert each .mdb once to SQLite (via `mdb-export` or access_parser), record the SHA-256 of the source .mdb and of the SQLite snapshot, and work from the snapshot; this makes every downstream query reproducible and avoids Windows/ODBC dependencies.
- Prefer MIT/BSD/Apache packages in the core path (skyfield, sgp4, shapely, geopandas, h3, bm25s, docling, pdfplumber, ruptures). Keep GPL/AGPL packages (pycraf, PyMuPDF, mdbtools-as-CLI) at arm's length (subprocess or optional) so the team's code can stay permissively licensed; note that calling `mdb-export` as a subprocess does not make the Python code a derivative work in most readings, but confirm if Traficom wants to reuse the code.
- Because eeveetza's implementations carry no licence file, treat them as reference material for validating your own P.1546/P.452 implementation rather than as dependencies.

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether ITU publishes an API or terms of use for scraping SpaceExplorer/SNL; the SNS database is TIES/subscription-gated.
- mdbtools' handling of the specific column types in IFIC .mdb files was not tested here.

## Key Question 3: Architecture patterns for auditable AI (provenance, deterministic core, schema-constrained output, run manifests, audit logs, human review states, agentic frameworks, governance guidance)

### Takeaway
The converging pattern across API vendors, agent frameworks and public-sector guidance is: deterministic code computes every number and produces evidence records with stable IDs; the LLM is restricted to grammar-constrained JSON whose citations must resolve to those IDs; every run writes a manifest (input hashes, code/model/prompt versions, parameters) and an append-only event log; humans hold explicit review states, which also keeps the tool on the "assistive" side of Finnish and EU rules on automated decisions.

### Cited Findings

Separating calculation from language
- PAL (Program-aided Language Models, arXiv 2211.10435, 2022/ICML 2023, older) showed that LLMs "often make logical and arithmetic mistakes in the solution part, even when the problem is decomposed correctly", and that delegating execution to a Python interpreter gave +15% absolute over chain-of-thought on GSM8K and far smaller degradation on GSM-HARD — [arXiv 2211.10435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10435)
- SPECTRA-GOV frames the contribution of an auditable regulatory architecture as "the integration of policy representation, enforcement, auditability, and provenance" with no LLM in the decision path — [arXiv 2609.29571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29571)

Schema-constrained outputs and grounded citations
- Anthropic's structured outputs (public beta, header `structured-outputs-2025-11-13`) offer JSON outputs via `output_format` and strict tool use via `strict: true`; strict mode "uses grammar-constrained sampling to ensure that the tool name is always one of the tools you provided, and that the tool input always conforms to the tool's input_schema"; limitations include requiring `additionalProperties: false` and no numeric or string-length constraints — [Anthropic docs, structured outputs](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/structured-outputs); [Vertex AI partner docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/partner-models/claude/structured_outputs)
- Anthropic's Citations feature chunks user-supplied documents "into sentences" and returns, per cited claim, the quoted text and its character-level location in the source; citation blocks cannot be cached directly but the source documents can — [Anthropic docs, citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/build-with-claude/citations); [Anthropic launch article](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-citations-api)
- The ALCE benchmark (EMNLP 2023) found that even strong models lacked complete citation support in roughly half of ELI5 generations (49–50%), motivating automatic citation precision/recall metrics — [ALCE / arXiv 2305.14627](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14627); [GitHub princeton-nlp/ALCE (MIT)](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE)

Provenance and run manifests
- W3C PROV-DM models provenance as entities (e.g., a file or dataset), activities (e.g., running an analysis), and agents (people, software, organisations) connected by generation, usage, derivation and association relations; PROV records "someone's account of what happened, not an absolute fact" — [W3C PROV primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-primer/); [prov library docs](https://prov.readthedocs.io/en/latest/explanation/prov-dm.html)
- OpenTelemetry's GenAI semantic conventions define a `gen_ai.*` namespace with a span tree of `invoke_agent` > `chat` > `execute_tool`, and content capture (prompts, tool schemas, arguments, results) is opt-in; sources conflict on whether the conventions are stable or still "Development" status in 2026 — [OpenTelemetry blog 2026](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/); [spec](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/gen-ai/)
- Reproducibility tooling examples: ReproLLM records model revisions, tokenizer/chat-template hashes, prompt hashes and generation parameters and has a `diff` command to explain why two runs differ; the R package localLLM hashes model identifier + prompt + parameters as an input hash, hashes outputs, and writes a timestamped audit log with a session-level SHA-256 — [ReproLLM on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/reprollm/0.5.0a1/); [localLLM reproducible-output vignette](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/localLLM/vignettes/reproducible-output.html)

Human-in-the-loop and agent frameworks
- LangGraph's official human-in-the-loop docs list "Approve or reject" as a core pattern: a node calls `interrupt()`, the pending payload surfaces under `__interrupt__`, and resuming uses `Command(resume=...)`; a checkpointer is required so the graph can pause and resume — [LangGraph HITL docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/human-in-the-loop); [LangChain changelog](https://changelog.langchain.com/announcements/interrupt-simplifying-human-in-the-loop-agents)
- The Claude Agent SDK (Python, MIT) evaluates permissions with hooks first ("allow, deny, or pass the request to the next step"), recommends `disallowed_tools` to block tools, supports custom in-process tools via `@tool` and `create_sdk_mcp_server`, and hooks are "a Python function that the Claude Code application invokes at specific points in the agent loop" — [claude-agent-sdk-python](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-agent-sdk-python); [permissions guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/permissions)

Governance guidance relevant to a Finnish public-sector deployer
- EU AI Act Article 14(4) requires that human overseers of high-risk systems be enabled "to properly understand the relevant capacities and limitations", "to remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output" (automation bias), "to correctly interpret the high-risk AI system's output", "to decide, in any particular situation, not to use the high-risk AI system" or override it, and "to intervene in the operation" or stop it — [AI Act Article 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)
- EU AI Act Article 50 (applies from 2 August 2026) requires deployers to disclose AI-generated text "published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest" except "where the AI-generated content has undergone a process of human review or editorial control" and "a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the publication" — [AI Act Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Finland's implementation bill (Government Proposal 46/2025, submitted 8 May 2025) uses a decentralised supervision model; Traficom "will act as the single point of contact under the EU AI Act", coordinates national authorities and supervises the AI sandbox; the proposal states "the public sector cannot use AI systems for automated decision-making due to national legislation" — [Hannes Snellman, 13 May 2025](https://www.hannessnellman.com/news-and-views/blog/eu-ai-act-latest-regulatory-developments-in-finland)
- Finland's general rules on automated decision-making in public administration took effect on 1 May 2023 and permit automation only where "making the decision does not require individual consideration"; automation "used only to assist a human decision" falls outside the statutory definition; authorities must disclose their automated procedures — [PRH, automated decision-making](https://prh.fi/en/presentation_and_duties/automated-decision-making.html); [Nordic report on Finland](https://pub.norden.org/temanord2024-503/finland.html)
- The UK Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS) is mandatory for UK central government and recommended across the public sector; its template has Tier 1 (a single "Summary Information" sheet) and Tier 2 ("split across eight further sheets") covering how the tool works and what data it uses; template v4.0 is published as an Excel file — [ATRS hub](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-hub); [ATRS template page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/algorithmic-transparency-template); [ATRS guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-for-organisations-using-the-algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-guidance-for-public-sector-bodies)
- NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile) treats content provenance (origin, transformation history, disclosure status) and transparency to users as mitigations under its information-integrity risk; secondary sources say it can assist with Article 50 obligations — [casrai summary](https://casrai.org/news/nist-ai-rmf-generative-ai-profile-ai-600-1-explained); [NIST AI 600-1 official PDF (not opened here)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
- The ICO/Alan Turing Institute guidance "Explaining decisions made with AI" (May 2020, older, non-binding) defines six explanation types, with the "rationale explanation" being "the reasons that led to an AI-assisted decision, which are to be delivered in an accessible and non-technical way", including "translating any mathematical rationale into plain language" — [SCL summary](https://www.scl.org/11914-explaining-decisions-made-with-ai-ico-and-the-alan-turing-institute-guidance-published/)

### Inferences
- A concrete evidence model for the hackathon: `EvidenceID = <source>:<ific_no>:<table>:<primary_key>[:<column>]` for structured data (e.g., `IFIC3074:com_el:123456:freq_min`), and `<doc_id>@p<page>#<provision>` for documents (e.g., `RR2024@p87#9.52`). Every LLM output field that asserts a fact must carry a list of EvidenceIDs; a validator rejects outputs whose numbers do not appear verbatim in the referenced evidence records. This is cheap to implement and directly answers the brief's "auditable citation/provenance" criterion.
- The run manifest should be a single JSON written per run: SHA-256 of each input file (IFIC .mdb, M4 OData snapshot, special-interest XLS), git commit of the code, model ID and prompt-template hash, parameters (thresholds, seeds), timestamps, and the SHA-256 of the output bundle. OpenTelemetry-style spans are optional; a JSONL event log with the same IDs is enough for a demo.
- Under Finnish law the tool must remain assistive: the final "send/dismiss" must be a named human action, recorded with reviewer, timestamp and reason. This also removes the Article 50 disclosure trigger for published text (human review and editorial responsibility), although Traficom may still choose to disclose internally.
- The EU AI Act Article 14 list is a ready-made checklist for the review UI even if the system is not high-risk: show capacities/limitations, warn about automation bias, make outputs interpretable (evidence links), allow override/dismiss, allow stop.
- Publish an ATRS-style Tier-2 record (owner, purpose, scope, data, model, human oversight, risks) in the repo README; this is the cheapest way to demonstrate "auditability and evidence quality" to judges.

## Key Question 4: Ingestion of ITU PDFs and regulations (tools, chunking with stable citations, change detection)

### Takeaway
ITU PDFs (RR appendices, circular letters, WRS slide decks) are text-based and parse cleanly with pypdf/pdfplumber; docling (MIT) is the best open option when page/bounding-box provenance or tables are needed, while PyMuPDF is AGPL/commercial; chunk regulations by provision number (e.g., "No. 9.52") with page numbers attached, and do version change detection with plain text diffs keyed on provision IDs.

### Cited Findings
- Docling's DoclingDocument is a Pydantic model that records provenance such as page numbers and document origin and stores bounding boxes when available; the official "visual grounding" example reads each element's first provenance item to get page number and bounding box, normalises it to page size and draws it on the page image — the reliable pattern for page-plus-box citations — [Docling paper arXiv 2501.17887](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17887v1); [Docling visual grounding example](https://docling-project.github.io/docling/examples/visual_grounding)
- Docling's chunking docs: HierarchicalChunker "creates one chunk for each detected document element" and attaches headers and captions; HybridChunker applies tokenizer-aware split and merge passes (`merge_peers=True` default, `repeat_table_header=True`); `contextualize()` returns a "potentially metadata-enriched serialization of the chunk" for embedding or generation; the page does not document the exact per-chunk `prov` field names — [Docling chunking concepts](https://docling-project.github.io/docling/concepts/chunking/)
- A vendor benchmark (Ertas, which sells a docling-based pipeline; treat as directional) reports table extraction of 97.9% (Docling), 93.4% (Unstructured), 91.7% (Marker); Marker leads multi-column reading order (96.1% vs 94.2%) and speed (6.1 vs 3.2 pages/s); Docling installs at over 1 GB and was far slower than lighter libraries in an independent 2025 benchmark — [Ertas blog (vendor)](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/pdf-parsing-accuracy-benchmark-docling-unstructured); [dev.to 2025 benchmark](https://dev.to/nhirschfeld/i-benchmarked-4-python-text-extraction-libraries-2025-4e7j)
- Licences (PyPI, 6 Nov 2026): docling MIT; pdfplumber MIT; pymupdf "Dual Licensed - GNU AFFERO GPL 3.0 or Artifex Commercial"; marker-pdf Apache-2.0 (code); unstructured Apache-2.0 — [PyPI JSON API, e.g. pymupdf](https://pypi.org/pypi/pymupdf/json)
- Empirical note from this research: `pypdf` 7.0.0 extracted clean text from the ITU Appendix 10 form (2 pages), Circular Letters CR/269, CR/322 and CR/526, the CR/C/2313 special-section cover page, and WRS-24 slide decks; the HTML-converting fetch tool failed on all of them (binary streams). Table-heavy ITU PDFs (e.g., the WRS-24 "Commenting on Special Sections at a glance" table) lost column structure in pypdf output — [Appendix 10 PDF](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/tpr/Documents/Appendix10-RR2020.pdf); [WRS-24 commenting deck](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/seminars/wrs/Documents/2024/Space-Workshops/Commenting%20General-WRS-24(KS).pdf)
- The KAUST RR-RAG paper extracted text from the ITU PDFs and "split into paragraphs"; fixed 150-word chunks performed "very poorly, with near-zero accuracy at top-3 and top-5", while 700-word chunks with top-7 retrieval reached 97% retrieval accuracy — [arXiv 2509.09651](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09651v2)
- Practitioner guidance for regulatory RAG notes that fixed-size chunking "can split regulatory rules across chunks, which hurts recall" — [dev.to RAGAS write-up](https://dev.to/michael_pham018/rag-evaluation-with-ragas-faithfulness-context-recall-and-answer-relevance-5cb)
- The ITU BR IFIC (Space) ships, per CR/269, databases named "IFICnnnn.mdb and SPS_ALL_IFICnnnn.mdb"; SpaceCom reads "IFIC.mdb in BR IFIC DVD" to list the CR/Cs an administration may comment on — [CR/269 (PDF)](https://itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/md/00/cr/cir/R00-CR-CIR-0269!!PDF-E.pdf); [Hon Fai Ng, Comments by Administrations (PDF, 2014, older)](https://itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/workshops/cyprus-2014/Documents/Presentations/Hon%20Fai%20Ng%20-%2004%20Comments%20by%20Administrations.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the RR, the Preface and ECC/Finnish regulation PDFs, do not chunk by fixed token windows; chunk by the document's own numbering (RR provision numbers "9.xx", Appendix tables, M4 table rows, ECC Report section numbers) and attach `page` from the parser. The provision number is the citation a Traficom expert expects; the page number is what the auditor needs.
- Use pypdf/pdfplumber for text-only documents (fast, MIT) and docling only when tables or bounding boxes are needed (e.g., Appendix 5 Table 5-1, Appendix 7). Avoid PyMuPDF unless AGPL is acceptable.
- Change detection between document versions needs no special tooling: normalise text per provision ID and diff with Python's `difflib` (standard library), reporting added/removed/changed provisions; store the SHA-256 of each source PDF in the run manifest so a reviewer can tell which edition an explanation cited — [Python difflib docs](https://docs.python.org/3/library/difflib.html)

### Gaps
- No independent, document-type-specific benchmark of PDF parsers on ITU regulatory PDFs exists; the numbers above are vendor-reported.
- The exact per-chunk provenance field names in docling's HybridChunker output were not confirmed from official docs (check `docling-core` chunk classes).

## Key Question 5: RAG over regulations with citations (retrieval with spans, faithfulness evaluation, small-corpus approaches, presenting "why" to experts)

### Takeaway
For a corpus of a few thousand provisions, lexical BM25 (bm25s, MIT) with provision-level chunks and glossary/abbreviation expansion is sufficient and fully reproducible; generate answers with citations constrained to retrieved provision IDs (or the Anthropic Citations API for sentence-level spans), and evaluate with citation precision/recall plus a small expert-adjudicated set, because semantic-similarity metrics do not predict legal correctness.

### Cited Findings
- BM25S (arXiv 2407.03618, 2024) is pure Python on SciPy sparse matrices, reports "up to 500x" speedups over rank-bm25 and over 100x on 10 of 14 BEIR datasets, defaults to Lucene scoring, needs no PyTorch; stemming is optional via `bm25s[full]` — [arXiv 2407.03618](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03618v1); [bm25s on PyPI (MIT)](https://pypi.org/project/bm25s)
- Telco-RAG's two cheap tricks for telecom text are glossary-based abbreviation expansion before retrieval and a router that narrows the search to the relevant document series; prompt formatting alone added about 4.6% accuracy — [Telco-RAG arXiv 2404.15939](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15939)
- The KAUST pipeline shows dense MiniLM+FAISS retrieval over RR paragraphs reaching 97% retrieval accuracy at 700-word/top-7, with an LLM reranker adding only +1% absolute at ~1.5x cost — [arXiv 2509.09651](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09651v2)
- Anthropic's Citations returns exact quoted spans with character-level locations in supplied documents — [Anthropic citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/build-with-claude/citations)
- Anthropic's "Contextual Retrieval" method (prepending chunk-specific context before indexing, combining BM25 and embeddings) is a vendor blog post not fetched in this research (unverified) — [Anthropic contextual retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- An IJCAI 2026 paper on Brazilian civil procedure found that "explicit article citation is the strongest predictor of legal correctness, and that semantic similarity metrics alone are insufficient", using an LLM-as-judge validated against expert assessment — [IJCAI 2026 paper 659](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/659)
- A 2026 preprint on regulatory-compliance QA builds its benchmark around the full set of references a correct answer needs ("citation closure") and per-rule attribution — [arXiv 2605.29742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29742); a 2026 systems paper on citation-enforced RAG over IRS/state tax documents reports improved citation fidelity — [arXiv 2603.14170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14170)
- CrossSource is an open harness measuring citation precision, citation recall and RAGAS-style faithfulness on public case law with a failure taxonomy (early, self-reported v0.1 results) — [GitHub zoeb-nomi/crosssource](https://github.com/zoeb-nomi/crosssource)
- ALCE's automatic citation-quality metrics correlate with human judgments; code is MIT — [ALCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14627)
- RAGAS faithfulness is the common off-the-shelf metric, but practitioner write-ups note it does not check whether each cited pinpoint supports the claim — [dev.to RAGAS](https://dev.to/michael_pham018/rag-evaluation-with-ragas-faithfulness-context-recall-and-answer-relevance-5cb)
- The ICO/Turing "rationale explanation" standard: reasons for the decision in plain language, with mathematical rationale translated — [SCL summary (2020, older)](https://www.scl.org/11914-explaining-decisions-made-with-ai-ico-and-the-alan-turing-institute-guidance-published/)

### Inferences
- The RR plus the BR IFIC Preface plus Finnish M4 plus a handful of ECC documents is a small corpus (low thousands of provision-level chunks). A vector database adds nothing; a bm25s index built at startup from a hashed corpus snapshot is reproducible and explainable (you can show the matched terms). If embeddings are added, keep BM25 as the primary and use embeddings only to re-rank.
- Build an abbreviation/glossary table (GSO, NGSO, FSS, MSS, pfd, EPFD, ESIM, CR/C, API/A, AP30B, 9.11A, HCM, M4 class names) and expand it in queries; this is the single most effective trick in the telecom-RAG literature.
- Generate explanations with a two-column structure Traficom experts can scan: "Why flagged" (deterministic rule hits with evidence IDs and computed values) and "Regulatory basis" (quoted provision text with provision number and page). Let the LLM write the prose only after both columns exist, and validate that every provision number in the prose is in the retrieved set.
- Measure citation precision (does each cited provision support the sentence?) and citation recall (is every needed provision cited?) on 20–30 expert-checked cases; report these alongside case-recognition precision/recall.

### Gaps
- No RR-specific citation-faithfulness benchmark exists; the KAUST dataset is MCQ accuracy without citations.
- No source covered how Finnish-language regulation (M4) interacts with English RR text in a single index; expect to need bilingual glossary entries.

## Key Question 6: Anomaly and trend detection on bi-weekly notice streams (methods and published filing-trend analyses)

### Takeaway
Keep it simple and explainable: per-IFIC counts by administration, band, service/station class, provision and bandwidth against a robust baseline (median/MAD or Poisson/negative-binomial rate), with change-point detection via ruptures only if a multi-IFIC history is available; the published record of filing surges (over one million satellites filed 2017–2022; six administrations filing >1,000/>10,000-satellite constellations in 2023; CR/C submissions roughly doubling 2017–2022) gives concrete, citable examples of the kind of anomalies worth flagging.

### Cited Findings
- Falle, Wright, Boley and Byers (Science 382, 150–152, 2023, DOI 10.1126/science.adi4639) used "the ITU's 'as received' database" to extract filings for constellations with ten or more satellites and found "more than 300 constellations representing over one million satellites were filed between 1 January 2017 and 31 December 2022 ... over 115 times the number of operational satellites currently in orbit"; more than 90 constellations exceed 1,000 satellites, 23 exceed 5,000, eight exceed 10,000; the largest single filing is Cinnamon-937 with 337,320 satellites (Rwanda, Sept 2021); most constellations were filed by China (~65) and the United States (~45), with >10,000-satellite constellations also filed by Rwanda, Germany, Spain, Norway, France and Solomon Islands; the authors "attempted to exclude repeated filings for the same satellites" — [Falle et al., accepted version (PDF)](https://outerspaceinstitute.ca/docs/One%20million%20(paper)%20satellites%20-%20Accepted%20Version%20.pdf)
- ITU's 2024 statement to COPUOS-STSC reports four trends for 2023: GSO notices at the notification stage fell for the first year in 2023 after steady growth to 2022; non-GSO submissions "continue to rise steadily since 2019"; in 2023 "six administrations submitted satellite projects as Advance Publications (type 'A') announcing coordination needs for constellations with more than 1,000 and even more than 10,000 satellites"; three new administrations submitted NGSO systems and "to date, 90 administrations have submitted filings for non-geostationary systems" (translated from the French text) — [UNOOSA 2024 ITU statement (PDF)](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2024/Statements/15_ITU.pdf)
- BR's "Trends in non-planned satellite filings" deck (Kadyrov, 28 June 2023) charts API and CR/C submissions 2011–2023; chart labels extracted from the PDF read, for API: 840, 1211, 838, 1027, 1352, 942 (2011–2016) then 330, 308, 359, 255, 228, 270, 70 (2017–2023 H1), and for CR/C: 179, 241, 250, 307, 304, 314, 164, 332, 320, 335, 314, 444, 511 (2011–2023 H1), with the note "1 July 2016 no new API are accepted for satellite networks and systems subject to the coordination" (label-to-year mapping is my reading of extracted text; verify against the chart); it also lists complexity drivers: "Non-GSOs with tens of thousands of satellites to more than 485 000 satellites with multiple configurations (up to 5)", "14 cases received in 2019-2023. Average treatment time 1 year" for No. 9.27 modifications, and "More submissions in the frequency bands where coordination with terrestrial services is required (Nos. 9.14, 9.21) in the frequency bands 1.5 GHz, 1.6 GHz, 2.4 GHz etc." — [ITU trends deck (PDF)](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/study-groups/rsg4/Documents/Presentations/Workshop-ITU-in-Service-of-Space_Trends_v2.pdf)
- Resolution 35 (WRC-19) imposes NGSO deployment milestones of 10% within 2 years, 50% within 5 years and 100% within 7 years after the end of the regulatory period; a Hogan Lovells survey counted 384 filings apparently subject to Resolution 35 (with the caveat that operators file multiple networks per constellation); ITU rejected a blanket one-year extension in 2023 — [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=130408); [ITU Resolution 35 page](https://itu.int:443/en/ITU-R/space/Pages/res35main.aspx); [ITU news 2020](https://www.itu.int/hub/2020/01/itu-members-agree-to-new-milestones-for-non-geostationary-satellite-deployment/)
- HITS statistics: ~25,000 Appendix 9/10 reports and ~10,500 communications since 2005 across 123 administrations; BR's chart shows yearly counts 2011–2023 by reporting and involved administrations — [WRS-24 HITS deck](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/seminars/wrs/Documents/2024/Presentations/31-HITS_Saman%20Jalayerian.pdf)
- Methods: ruptures provides offline change-point detection with parametric cost functions under penalty or budget constraints (2018, older); gridcp provides online Poisson-rate change detection via an exponential-family GLR score; no single Python library combining count-data baselines with change-point detection was found — [ruptures arXiv 1801.00826](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00826); [gridcp arXiv 2608.18695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18695)
- Z-score baselines assume near-normal data and are distorted by the outliers they seek; Isolation Forest is non-parametric and multivariate but its results depend on sampling, seed, features and threshold, making it less reproducible than a z-score rule; one comparison recommends pairing Isolation Forest with simpler z-score/IQR rules — [Kanaries comparison (low-authority)](https://ml.kanaries.net/docs/guides/outlier-methods); [scikit-learn IsolationForest docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.IsolationForest.html)
- ITU SpaceExplorer already provides dashboards to "monitor the latest BR IFIC (Space) publications, and analyse orbit and spectrum occupancy" — [ITU hub](https://www.itu.int/hub/2022/09/itu-space-explorer-satellite-data-dashboards/)

### Inferences
- With only IFIC 3074 and 3076 in hand, true time-series anomaly detection is impossible; what is possible and defensible is (a) within-IFIC distributional anomalies (an administration or network with an unusual number of groups/emissions, unusual bandwidths, or many bands touching Finnish special-interest frequencies) and (b) comparison against published baselines (the 2011–2023 counts above, the 2017–2022 constellation statistics). If Traficom can provide several earlier IFICs, add per-IFIC counts and a robust z-score (median/MAD) or Poisson/negative-binomial rate test with ruptures for change points.
- Every anomaly flag should be expressed as a sentence with the numbers it used ("Administration X filed 7 CR/C networks in this IFIC vs median 1 over the last 12 IFICs"), computed deterministically; the LLM should only rephrase, never compute.
- Resolution 35 milestone dates and "as received" vs "published" counts (deduplicating networks per constellation, as Falle et al. did) are the two pitfalls to document in the methodology.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed analysis applying anomaly detection to BR IFIC notice streams was found.
- Annual total filing counts for 2024–2025 were not found in primary sources (the 2025 UNOOSA statement URL returned 404).

## Key Question 7: Structure and wording of inter-administration correspondence (9.52 disagreement, 9.41 inclusion, 9.21 objection, 9.3 comments, AP30 Article 4, Article 15/Appendix 10, T/R 25-08) with public examples and template fields

### Takeaway
The RR text, BR circular letters and the CR/C special-section cover page define the exact content a comment must carry: identification of the special section and IFIC, the provision commented under, the commenting administration's own assignments/networks on which the disagreement is based, technical justification (e.g., ΔT/T > 6% for 9.41/9.7; pfd excess or visible recorded terrestrial stations for 9.11/9.14/9.21/C), suggestions for resolution, and copies to both the notifying administration and the Bureau before the four-month deadline; SpaceCom structures the ITU-facing part, but the letter to the notifying administration "together with reasons" remains a separate obligation and is the natural LLM-drafting target.

### Cited Findings

Regulatory text and deadlines
- RR No. 9.52 (as quoted in a BR workshop deck): "If an administration, following its action under No. 9.50, does not agree to the request for coordination, it shall, within four months of the date of publication of the BR IFIC under No. 9.38, or of the date of dispatch of the coordination data under No. 9.29, inform the requesting administration of its disagreement and shall provide information concerning its own assignments upon which that disagreement is based. It shall also make such suggestions as it is able to offer with a view to satisfactory resolution of the matter. A copy of that information shall be sent to the Bureau." No. 9.52C: an administration not responding within the same four-month period "shall be regarded as unaffected and, in the cases of Nos. 9.11 to 9.14, the provisions of Nos. 9.48 and 9.49 shall apply" — [Hon Fai Ng, Comments by Administrations (ITU, 2014, older)](https://itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/workshops/cyprus-2014/Documents/Presentations/Hon%20Fai%20Ng%20-%2004%20Comments%20by%20Administrations.pdf)
- BR's WRS-24 commenting deck defines a comment as "An indication from an Administration on whether or not it accepts the technical characteristics of a frequency assignment to a satellite network submitted to the Bureau"; administrations comment "To protect their radiocommunication services against potential interference" or "To exclude the territory of an Administration from the service area"; "All Administrations may need to comment ... even if they are not identified as potentially affected in Special Section"; comments are due "Within 4 months from the date of publication of the corresponding Special Section"; they go "To the notifying Administration By correspondence" and "To the Bureau By correspondence and e-Submission (SpaceCom comments file)"; criteria for comments when not identified: "(1) Interference to terrestrial services (2) Interference to space services (3) Exclusion of territory"; SpaceCom v10 is used for IFIC 3037 (7.1.2025) onward; comment files must be complete and follow a naming pattern such as "CRE-IFIC2974_SUI-001.mdb" — [WRS-24 Commenting on Special Sections (PDF)](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/seminars/wrs/Documents/2024/Space-Workshops/Commenting%20General-WRS-24(KS).pdf)
- The same deck's "at a glance" table: API/A comments under No. 9.3 require SpaceCom (summary published as API/B); CR/C under 9.7/9.7A/9.7B disagreements under No. 9.52 do not require SpaceCom ("Corresp."), with BR assistance under Nos. 9.60–9.62; CR/C under 9.11, 9.11A (9.12, 9.12A, 9.13, 9.14) and 9.21/A/B/C disagreements under No. 9.52 require SpaceCom (summary CR/D); No. 9.41 inclusion requests require SpaceCom (summary CR/E); AP30/30A Regions 1&3 Article 4 comments (§ 4.1.7, 4.1.9, 4.1.10) are by correspondence; Region 2 (§ 4.2.10, 4.2.13, 4.2.14) via SpaceCom; AP30B § 6.10 by correspondence — [WRS-24 Commenting deck](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/seminars/wrs/Documents/2024/Space-Workshops/Commenting%20General-WRS-24(KS).pdf)

### Inferences
- A draft-correspondence template for the space side needs these fields, all of which can be filled deterministically from the IFIC database plus Finnish data before any LLM prose: (1) addressee (notifying administration symbol and, for ITU, brmail/e-Communications) and Traficom signatory/contact; (2) special section ID (e.g., CR/C/xxxx or API/A/xxxx), BR IFIC number and date, network name, notifying administration, orbital position or NGSO identifier, BR identification number; (3) the provision(s) under which Finland acts (9.52 disagreement under 9.11/9.11A/9.14/9.21; 9.41 inclusion under 9.7/9.7A/9.7B; 9.3 comment on API/A; AP30/30A Article 4 comment; 23.13B/C territory exclusion); (4) the Finnish assignments/networks "upon which that disagreement is based" (station class, frequency range, location, licence/M4 reference, special-interest list row); (5) the technical reason with values (frequency overlap in MHz, computed pfd at the Finnish site vs the applicable threshold, ΔT/T if 9.7, distance/visibility), each tied to an evidence ID and calculator version; (6) "suggestions ... with a view to satisfactory resolution" (e.g., exclude Finnish territory from service area, limit pfd, coordinate bilaterally); (7) the deadline statement referencing No. 9.52/9.52C and the four-month expiry date computed from the IFIC date; (8) a note that a SpaceCom comment file (named per BR convention) is submitted via e-Submission and that this letter is the copy required "together with reasons"; (9) attachments list.
- For the terrestrial side (IFIC 3076 land-mobile notices), the analogous deliverables are (a) a bilateral coordination request/objection to the neighbouring administration under the applicable agreement (T/R 25-08 or bilateral) with the HCM-style parameters (frequency, location, ERP, antenna height/pattern, field strength at the border), and (b) where interference is actually experienced, an Appendix 10 report with items a–x in letter order, ready for upload to HITS. The tool should emit Appendix 10 as a structured record mapping each letter to an evidence ID.
- Because SpaceCom already "includes a facility to create reports" (CR/269) and e-Communications now carries correspondence, the hackathon tool should output content in both forms: a structured JSON mirroring SpaceCom's fields (provision, network, groups, justification) and a human-readable letter. Do not try to generate the SpaceCom .mdb itself in 48 hours.

## Key Question 8: Evaluation strategy for case-recognition accuracy without many labelled examples (quasi-ground truth, precision/recall framing, "material" errors, calibration, reproducibility)

### Takeaway
Build layered quasi-labels (BR-identified affected administrations containing FIN as the "hard" positives; rule-derived candidates as "soft" positives; synthetic injected cases for recall), keep a small expert-adjudicated reference set untouched by any rule, report precision/recall with uncertainty and a separate "material" error count tied to the four-month deadline and technical margin, and show calibration of priority scores with a reliability table; reproducibility is demonstrated by pinned input hashes, fixed seeds and a re-run that reproduces identical output hashes.

### Cited Findings
- Weak-supervision evaluation: Polo et al. (NeurIPS 2024) bound accuracy, precision, recall and F1 without ground truth via partial identification (Fréchet bounds), because these metrics "can't be computed directly without ground truth"; Joyce et al.'s "Approximate Ground Truth Refinements" estimate precision/recall bounds over partially labelled data (cited in Firenze) — [arXiv 2312.04601](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04601); [Firenze arXiv 2207.00827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00827)
- Practical guidance: keep "a small, adjudicated human-labeled reference set that no labeling function touched", track per-rule precision and coverage, and size slices with a few hundred adjudicated examples where possible — [Arize glossary (practitioner source)](https://arize.com/glossary/weak-supervision/)
- Injected-case recall estimates are biased by what you inject: "by injecting bugs of a type that are harder or easier for a given tool to detect, one can influence the measured recall" — [arXiv 1901.02819 (older)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.02819)
- LLM-as-judge: Zheng et al. (2023) reported GPT-4 agreement above 80% with human preferences; Krumdick et al. (2025, "No Free Labels") found judges agree with experts mainly on questions they can answer themselves, and that supplying human-written reference answers improves agreement and reduces self-preference bias; expert domains showed agreement as low as 64–68% — [arXiv 2503.05061](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05061v2); [Zheng et al. 2023 (arXiv 2306.05685)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)
- Citation evaluation: ALCE-style citation precision/recall and the IJCAI 2026 finding that explicit article citation predicts legal correctness — [ALCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14627); [IJCAI 2026/659](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/659)
- Retrieval evaluation at the chunk level can use a ROUGE-L F1 threshold (0.7 in the KAUST paper) between retrieved and gold passages — [arXiv 2509.09651](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09651v2)
- Calibration of scores: Guo et al. (2017, older) introduced expected calibration error and reliability diagrams and showed modern classifiers are often miscalibrated; temperature scaling is a simple fix — [arXiv 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)
- The regulatory meaning of a false negative on the space side: an administration not responding under No. 9.52 within four months "shall be regarded as unaffected" (No. 9.52C) — [Hon Fai Ng deck](https://itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/workshops/cyprus-2014/Documents/Presentations/Hon%20Fai%20Ng%20-%2004%20Comments%20by%20Administrations.pdf)
- BR's own 9.36 examination identifies affected administrations using pfd thresholds over territories or "Recorded terrestrial stations with frequency overlap and visible", and lists potentially affected administrations under 9.36.1 "for information only" — [Hon Fai Ng deck, pp. 17–20](https://itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/workshops/cyprus-2014/Documents/Presentations/Hon%20Fai%20Ng%20-%2004%20Comments%20by%20Administrations.pdf); [WRS-24 Karina deck](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/seminars/wrs/Documents/2024/Presentations/20-Regulatory%20process%20for%20satellite%20systems%20subject%20to%20coordination%20C%20Karina.pdf)
- Reproducibility manifests: hash model/prompt/parameters (ReproLLM; localLLM) — [ReproLLM](https://pypi.org/project/reprollm/0.5.0a1/); [localLLM vignette](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/localLLM/vignettes/reproducible-output.html)

### Inferences
- Quasi-ground-truth tiers for IFIC 3074 (space): Tier A ("BR says so"): special sections where FIN appears in the 9.36 coordination-requirement tables or 9.36.1 potentially-affected lists, or where Finnish networks appear in 9.36.2 lists. Tier B ("rules say so"): networks whose frequency groups overlap Finnish special-interest frequencies or M4 allocations of interest, with service area or beam covering Finland, or whose pfd at a Finnish coordinate exceeds the relevant threshold per the team's calculator. Tier C ("synthetic"): inject a handful of modified notices (shift a group into a Finnish band, move a service area to cover Finland) and verify they are caught. For IFIC 3076 (terrestrial): Tier A: notices from neighbouring administrations within a distance threshold of the Finnish border with frequency overlap with Finnish assignments; Tier B: field strength at the border above an agreed threshold; Tier C: injected notices.
- Report precision and recall separately per tier, with Wilson or bootstrap intervals (sample sizes will be small), and state explicitly that Tier A labels are necessary-but-not-sufficient (BR's identification "for information only" may include administrations that would not actually object).
- Define "material" errors operationally: a material false negative is a Tier A or Tier B case with a 9.52/9.41 deadline where no flag was raised; a material false positive is a flagged case whose evidence contains no Finnish assignment, no band overlap and no coverage of Finland (i.e., the expert would dismiss it in under a minute). Count these separately from benign ranking errors.
- Calibrate the priority score with a reliability table over the adjudicated set (bins of predicted priority vs fraction the expert agreed were worth action); even 30 cases in five bins gives judges a picture.
- Reproducibility demo: run the pipeline twice from the manifest and show identical output hashes; use temperature 0 and a fixed seed for the LLM step, but state that LLM determinism is not guaranteed and that the deterministic layer (which carries all numbers) is bit-for-bit reproducible.
- Use an LLM judge only for the prose quality of drafts (clarity, no claims beyond evidence), with reference-grounded prompts, and validate it against expert ratings on the adjudicated set; do not use it to label case relevance.

### Gaps
- No published precision/recall baseline exists for "cases relevant to administration X" screening; the team's numbers will be the first of their kind and should be framed as such.
- The exact fields in IFIC 3074/3076 that encode affected administrations and 9.36.1/9.36.2 lists are covered by the data-format researcher; the evaluation design assumes they are extractable.

## Implications for the hackathon build

1. Position the product as "the layer between the BR IFIC and SpaceCom/e-Communications/HITS": it ingests the IFIC snapshot, ranks cases for Finland with evidence, and drafts both the structured comment content and the letter to the notifying administration. Nothing on the market or in the literature does this; vendors stop at deterministic checks and the ITU stops at submission tooling.
2. Architecture to build (deterministic core, LLM wrapper): .mdb to SQLite snapshot with hashes; rule engine producing EvidenceIDs; calculators (band overlap, coverage/visibility, pfd/field strength) with version strings; LLM step restricted to strict JSON (Anthropic structured outputs or equivalent) whose every claim lists EvidenceIDs and provision IDs; validator that rejects unreferenced numbers or provisions; run manifest JSON; append-only JSONL event log; review states new/investigate/approve/dismiss with reviewer, timestamp and reason; export bundle (ranked cases CSV/JSON for IFIC 3074 and 3076, draft letters, manifest).
3. Reuse: mdbtools or access_parser; shapely/geopandas (and h3 only if hexagonal aggregation helps the map); skyfield/sgp4 only if a visibility check is needed; bm25s for RR/M4 retrieval; pypdf or docling for the RR and Preface; ruptures only if a multi-IFIC history is provided; pydantic for schemas; LangGraph `interrupt()` or the Claude Agent SDK hooks if an agent loop is wanted, but a plain orchestrator script is acceptable and more reproducible.
4. Skip: vector databases, model fine-tuning, full EPFD/S.1503, generating SpaceCom .mdb files, GUI polish beyond a review queue and an evidence drawer, and any "autonomous sending" (Finnish law requires the decision to remain human).
5. Evaluation deliverable: a markdown report with Tier A/B/C precision/recall and intervals, a material-error table, a reliability table for priority scores, citation precision/recall on 20–30 expert-checked explanations, and a second-run hash equality proof.
6. Governance deliverable: an ATRS-style Tier-2 record in the README (owner, purpose, scope, data sources with hashes, model and prompt versions, human oversight states, known limitations), a one-paragraph note on EU AI Act Article 50 (human review and editorial responsibility apply) and Article 14-style oversight affordances in the UI.
7. Correspondence deliverable: letter templates for 9.52 disagreement, 9.41 inclusion request, 9.3 API/A comment and Appendix 10 report, each with the field list above and a "reasons" section whose numbers are injected from calculators; include the computed four-month expiry date and the BR comment-file naming convention.
