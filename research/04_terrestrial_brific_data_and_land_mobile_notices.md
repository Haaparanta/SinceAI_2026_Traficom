# Terrestrial BR IFIC data and land-mobile notices (documentation-side research)

Research date: 6 November 2026. Scope: official ITU documentation (Preface, ReadMe, TerRaSys developer docs, Circular Letter CR/516, WRS seminar decks), the published Article 11 index of BR IFIC 3076, and a byte-level peek at the `brific3076.zip` central directory. The actual `.mdb` was **not** opened here (another researcher does that); every statement about what is *inside* the MDB tables is therefore marked as either documented (Preface/TerRaSys docs) or inferred.

Conventions used below: "Preface" = *Preface to the BR International Frequency Information Circular (Terrestrial Services)*, English, edition BR IFIC 3083 (the live file at the Preface URL is already the 3083 edition); page numbers are the printed page numbers in that PDF.

## Q1. What exactly is the free "MDB extract" (vs. the full BR IFIC ISO/DVD), what format and size, and what do ReadMe_3076 and the Preface say?

### Takeaway
`brific3076.zip` (2.4 MB) contains exactly one file, `brific3076.mdb` (25.1 MB uncompressed), a genuine MS Access database that the Bureau describes as an **extract of the assignments/notices included in that publication** — not a snapshot of the whole MIFR. The full terrestrial BR IFIC (ISO image / DVD, subscription) ships the complete TerRaBase, which since BR IFIC 3042 (18 March 2025) is a **SQLite** file `BRIFIC_nnnn.db3` plus `GLAD.db`, replacing (not updating) the previous issue's database.

### Inferences
- Size arithmetic supports the "extract" reading: 25 MB of Access file for 3,510 Article 11 notices plus ~490 Plan notices (≈ 6 KB per notice including antenna/coordination/remark child rows) is plausible; a full terrestrial MIFR would be orders of magnitude larger. Expect the MDB to hold **only the notices/assignments published in that issue** (Parts 1–3 and probably the Special-Section Parts A/B/C), plus reference/code tables.
- Because the extract is produced by the Bureau in Access format while the master database is now SQLite, the MDB's table layout is probably the **legacy ("TerRaBase 2019") structure** the converter produces (Preface Chapter III still lists `lat_deg/lat_min/lat_sec/lat_ns/long_deg/long_min/long_sec/long_ew` in `fxm_geo_pt`), but it could equally be a bespoke extract schema. The researcher opening the file must confirm table names before any code is written.
- The internal MDB file date (13 July) precedes publication (21 July) by eight days and the ReadMe is dated 9 July: the data cut-off of an issue is roughly 1–2 weeks before the publication date. Useful when reasoning about "date received" (`d_rcv`) distributions.

### Gaps
- No ITU document found that explicitly lists the tables/columns of the web MDB extract or states whether it contains Special-Section (Plan) notices as well as Article 11 notices. Must be verified by opening the file (`mdb-tables`).
- ISO/DVD price and the current online-access policy were not verified from a primary page.

## Q2. Where is the terrestrial database data dictionary, and what are the table names and key columns?

### Takeaway
Two official sources together form the data dictionary: Preface Chapter III §1 (pp. 10–28) maps every Appendix 4 data item to its notice element key (`t_*`) and its **database field name and table** (`fxm_terra`, `fxm_geo`, `fxm_geo_pt`, `fxm_ant`, `fxm_ant_hgt`, `fxm_ant_diag`, `fxm_rot`, `fxm_coord`, `fxm_fdg*`, `fxm_pub_hist`, `fxm_rmks`, `fxm_status`, …), and the TerRaSys developer docs (https://www.itu.int/ITU-R/terrasys/docs) give the 2025 SQLite schema including a full FXM entity-relationship diagram with column names and types.

- For a P.1546 field-strength calculation, FB/T12 (and FL, FX) notices are the easy case: a POINT transmitter with coordinates, `site_alt`, `hgt_agl`, `pwr_dbw` + `pwr_eiv` (I/E/V), `gain_max` + `gain_type`, `azm_max_e`, `bmwdth`, `polar`, optional `fxm_ant_hgt` (effective heights per azimuth) and `fxm_ant_diag` (pattern attenuation per azimuth). ML/T13 notices describe the uplink: the transmitter is a *population of mobiles inside a circle/zone* with a per-mobile e.i.r.p.; the field strength at the Finnish border must then be computed from the nearest point of the circle (worst case) with an assumed mobile antenna height (T/R 25-08 uses 3 m/10 m conventions, see Q6), and this assumption must be stated in the expert-reviewable output.
- The high FB/ML counts (SVN, KAZ, CHN, RUS) show that land-mobile notices are a large share of a typical issue, so filters by administration and geography are essential before any propagation run.
- Because Finland and its neighbours lie in the GE06 planning area, land-mobile assignments in 174–230 MHz and 470–862 MHz are notified on G11–G14 forms and can additionally pass through Special Section GE06L (Parts A/B/C, `pub_prefix` ≠ NTFD_RR) under Article 4 of GE06; they do also appear in Article 11 Parts 1–3 — the 3076 index contains RUS FB notices at 793.5 and 794.74 MHz (Ruskeala, Titovka) in Part 1 — so the pipeline must handle both channels and should not assume that GE06-band notices are absent from NTFD_RR (unverified whether the web MDB includes GE06L notices).

## Q4. What do Parts 1/2/3 (and IIB) mean, what are the notice actions (ADD/MODIFY/SUPPRESS/WITHDRAW), and what are the Special Sections RR9.21/C, /D, GE06, GE84, GE75?

### Takeaway
Part I = notices received (acknowledgement of receipt, before examination), Part II = notices with favourable findings being recorded in the MIFR, Part III = notices returned with unfavourable findings, Part IIB = recorded assignments whose findings were reviewed (RR 11.50/14.1); the "intent" of a Part I notice is ADD, MODIFY, SUPPRESS or WITHDRAW(N). RR9.21/C and /D are the terrestrial-side Special Sections for requests for agreement under RR 9.21 (C = request published under 9.38; D = status at expiry of the time limit, 9.52C); GE06/GE84/GE75 Special Sections carry Plan-modification procedures with Parts A (proposed), B (agreed/recorded) and C (cancelled).

## Q5. How to diff consecutive IFICs: stable identifiers, how MODIFY/SUPPRESS refer to the MIFR record, ReadMe counts

### Takeaway
Within one issue, the MDB already labels each published notice with its Part and intent (via `fxm_pub_hist.pub_no/pub_prefix/pub_part` and `fxm_terra.intent`); across issues, the Bureau's 9-digit assignment identifier (`assgn_id`, printed as "BR Id" in the index) is the key that persists from Part 1 through Part 2/3 and into the MIFR, while the administration's own `adm_ref_id` ties ADD→MODIFY→SUPPRESS of the same assignment and `target`/`fxm_target` points a MODIFY/SUPPRESS notice at the recorded assignment's `terrakey`. The ReadMe gives per-Part totals only (no per-administration breakdown); the Article 11 index PDF does give per-notice administration.

### Cited Findings
- `assgn_id`: "Assignment Identifier given to each assignment by the Bureau during the processing of a notice. This number is recorded in the Master Register" (Preface p. 25); `adm_ref_id`: "Unique identification code given by the administration to the assignment or allotment" (p. 10); `d_rcv` "date of receipt of the notice by the Bureau"; `d_recog` "Date of entry of the assignment in the Master Register" (pp. 26, 28) — [Preface EN](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/brific/BRIFIC/Preface/PREFACE_EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Recommended diff algorithm with only the free MDB extracts (N−1, N): (1) load both; (2) key every notice on `assgn_id` (fallback: `adm`+`adm_ref_id`; fallback: identifying tuple freq/coords/class/bandwidth/emission/hours); (3) classify rows of issue N by `pub_part` × `intent`: Part 1 ADD → "new notice received", Part 1 MODIFY → "modification received" (link to the targeted record via `fxm_target.target`/`fxm_terra.target` if the targeted row is present, else via `adm_ref_id`), SUPPRESS → "suppression requested", WITHDRAW → "withdrawn", Part 2 → "recorded (favourable)", Part 3 → "returned (unfavourable)"; (4) cross-issue: `assgn_id` in N Part 2/3 ∩ earlier issue Part 1 gives processing latency (`d_rcv` → `pub_no`); `assgn_id` in N−1 Part 1 but in neither N nor later issues is still under treatment (not a change). The internal `terrakey` may or may not be stable between extracts — do not rely on it until verified.
- Full MIFR-level diffs (what the complete Register for RUS/S/NOR looked like before vs after) require two full TerRaBase snapshots (ISO subscription or TerRaSys online); the free extracts give the *deltas* only. For the hackathon, a delta-only design is honest and sufficient for "new/modified/withdrawn".
- A SUPPRESS notice carries identifying items (frequency, coordinates, class — visible in the index for SUP rows) but not necessarily full technical data of the recorded assignment; to evaluate the interference *reduction* from a suppression you need the recorded record (previous extracts or MIFR).

### Gaps
- Not verified whether the same `assgn_id` is kept when a notice moves from Part 1 to Part 2/3 (strongly implied by Preface wording and TerRaQ workflows, but needs a two-issue check on real data, e.g. 3074/3075 Part 1 vs 3076 Part 2).
- Not verified whether the MDB extract of issue N includes the *targeted* recorded assignment rows for MODIFY/SUPPRESS notices or only the notice rows.

- Kostomuksha (≈30.8°E, 64.6°N) sits only a few tens of kilometres from the Finnish border near Kuhmo/Vartius (own estimate from coordinates); the 152 MHz FB/ML pair there is a textbook test case for the T/R 25-08 108–380 MHz threshold (12 dBµV/m at 10 m in 25 kHz).

- The 2025 amendment of T/R 25-08 was not read; thresholds above are from the 2018 edition.

## Q8. Reading the .mdb on macOS/Linux (mdbtools, access_parser, UCanAccess) and gotchas

### Takeaway
`brew install mdbtools` / `apt install mdbtools` gives `mdb-tables`, `mdb-schema`, `mdb-export`, `mdb-json`, `mdb-sql` and `mdb-count`, which is enough to dump every table to CSV/SQLite and load with pandas; `pip install access-parser` is a pure-Python read-only alternative. The extract is a Jet (`.mdb`) file, so no `.accdb`/ACE issues are expected.

### Cited Findings
- mdbtools: "a set of programs for reading Microsoft Access databases on *nix systems"; libmdb, libmdbsql, ODBC driver; utilities `mdb-ver` (JET 3 or JET 4), `mdb-tables`, `mdb-schema` (DDL), `mdb-export` (CSV or SQL), `mdb-json`, `mdb-count`, `mdb-sql`, `mdb-queries`; install `apt install mdbtools`, `brew install mdbtools`, `port install mdbtools`; "Write support is experimental"; `mdb-import` only from the build tree — [mdbtools GitHub README](https://github.com/mdbtools/mdbtools)
- access_parser: "pure-Python parser for Microsoft Access database files" handling `.mdb` and `.accdb` "without any external binary dependencies"; `pip install access-parser`; `AccessParser("/path/to/file.mdb")`, `.catalog` (tables), `.parse_table("table_name")` → `defaultdict(list)` by column; Apache-2.0; "tested on only a limited set of database files" — [access_parser GitHub README](https://github.com/claroty/access_parser)
- The zip holds a single `.mdb` (not `.accdb`): `brific3076.mdb` 25,145,344 bytes — own inspection of [brific3076.zip](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/brific/BRIFIC/MDB/brific3076.zip)
- Column types that will need care (from the Preface/diagram): `rmk_text varchar(max)` (an Access Memo), `site_name nvarchar(30)` (Unicode site names), boolean-like `varchar(5)` 'TRUE'/'FALSE' flags, `datetime2(3)` dates, DMS coordinate components (legacy) or `longitude_dms` floats (2025) — [Preface EN](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/brific/BRIFIC/Preface/PREFACE_EN.pdf); [FXM ER diagram](https://www.itu.int/ITU-R/terrasys/docs/assets/files/fxm-9d230ffa24930deb0b3652581e958646.png)
- Third-party importer experience: "Some systems fail to load because of missing data"; "two stations had an antenna height of zero metres, and only one had a valid height"; duplicate filings "identical in all parameters except one field" — [Transfinite: Import from the Terrestrial IFIC](https://docs.transfinite.com/interplanetary/user_guide_import_from_the_terrestrial_ific.html)

### Inferences
- Simplest robust pipeline (no Access, no Windows): `unzip` → `mdb-ver` → `mdb-tables -1 brific3076.mdb` → for each table `mdb-export -D '%Y-%m-%d' -T '%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S' brific3076.mdb <table>` → `pandas.read_csv` (keep `dtype=str` for codes such as `adm`, `assgn_id`, `bdwdth_cde`, `emi_cls`), or `mdb-schema brific3076.mdb sqlite | sqlite3 brific3076.db` + `mdb-export -I sqlite` to get a SQLite copy that mirrors the official SQLite layout. The exact `-D/-T` flag names should be checked with `man mdb-export` for the installed version (not confirmed from the README).
- Gotchas to expect: Memo remarks containing newlines/quotes (use a real CSV parser, never split on commas); non-ASCII site names (mdbtools converts Jet4 UCS-2 to UTF-8 via iconv by default — verify with a Russian/French site name); dates exported in US `MM/DD/YY` style unless a format is given; `'TRUE'/'FALSE'` text flags; floats for `freq_assgn` (unit presumably MHz — verify against `bdwdth_cde`/index); coordinates possibly split into deg/min/sec/hemisphere columns that must be recombined with sign; `radius` in km; `hgt_agl`/`site_alt`/`eff_hgt` in metres; `pwr_dbw` in dBW with type in `pwr_eiv`; gain type in `gain_type` (I/V/D — convert dBd to dBi by +2.15 dB when needed and say so).
- UCanAccess (Java JDBC) was not researched; mdbtools and access_parser cover the need.

### Gaps
- mdbtools option names/encoding behaviour were not verified from man pages during this research.

## Q10. How to download and how to detect a newly published terrestrial IFIC automatically

### Takeaway
All per-issue files have predictable URLs keyed on the four-digit IFIC number (`.../MDB/brific<NNNN>.zip`, `.../ReadMe/ReadMe_<NNNN>.pdf`, `.../Article11-Index/ific<NNNN>.pdf`, Special Sections under `.../SpecialSection/<RR921|GE06|GE84|GE75>/...`), issues are published every two weeks (Tuesdays: 3076 = 21 Jul 2026, 3083 = 27 Oct 2026, so 3084 is expected on 10 Nov 2026), and an HTTP HEAD on the next number returns `404 NOT FOUND` until the file exists (`brific3084.zip` → 404 on 6 Nov 2026, `brific3083.zip` → 200, 3,378,413 bytes), so a cron'd HEAD poller is sufficient; no RSS feed or mailing list for terrestrial IFIC releases was found.

### Cited Findings

## Implications for the hackathon build

1. **Data reality check first.** The free input is an Access `.mdb` extract of *the notices published in that issue* (~3.5 k Article 11 notices + Plan notices per issue), not the MIFR. Build the architecture around deltas: per issue, classify notices by `pub_part` × `intent`; across issues, chain them by `assgn_id` (BR Id) and `adm`+`adm_ref_id`. State explicitly that "withdrawn/suppressed" is known from the notices themselves and that MIFR context (what was previously recorded) would come from TerRaSys/eMIFR with a TIES account.
2. **Schema detection, not assumptions.** The master database changed to SQLite with renamed coordinate/target columns in BR IFIC 3042; the MDB may follow the legacy layout. Write the loader to inspect `mdb-schema` output and map either `lat_deg/lat_min/lat_sec/lat_ns…` (legacy) or `longitude_dms/long_dec` (2025) to decimal degrees; handle `target` either as a `fxm_terra` column or via `fxm_target`.
3. **Land-mobile filter = classes FB, ML, FL, MO (plus FX for context), notice types T12/T13/T14/G12/G13/G14.** Treat FB (point transmitters) and ML (uplink from a CIRCLE/ZONE of mobiles) differently in the propagation step; for ML use the nearest point of the circle to the border and an explicit mobile-height assumption (3 m, with the T/R 25-08 10 m→3 m 10 dB convention documented).
4. **Finland relevance from notice data:** `adm`/`ctry` ∈ {RUS, S, NOR, EST} (tier 1) and {LVA, LTU, DNK, POL, D, BLR} (tier 2); distance from the site/zone to the Finnish border within a band-dependent screening radius; `fxm_coord.adm='FIN'` as a direct flag; frequency within Finnish land-mobile bands. BR IFIC 3076 contains 294 RUS notices (45 FB, 62 ML, 176 FX, 11 BT) and zero S/NOR/EST notices; 36 of the RUS notices fall inside a Finland-adjacent box west of 36.5°E and north of 58.5°N (12 FB, 7 ML, 17 FX) at Kostomuksha, Lyaskelya, Vyborg, Kingisepp, Ruskeala and Titovka, in the 150–155 MHz, 414–426 MHz and 793–795 MHz ranges — so the demo should centre on RUS (e.g. the 152.025 MHz FB / 152.9 MHz ML pair at Kostomuksha, ~30–40 km from the border; the 153 MHz Vyborg base stations; the 424/414 MHz Kingisepp FB/ML modifications) and prove the "no relevant notices from S/NOR/EST in this issue" negative result honestly.
5. **Expert-reviewable findings** should carry, per notice: BR Id, adm, adm_ref_id, Part/intent, notice type, class, frequency, `bdwdth_cde`, `emi_cls`, site + coordinates (+ zone radius), `pwr_dbw`/`pwr_eiv`, `gain_max`/`gain_type`, `hgt_agl`, `site_alt`, `azm_max_e`, `bmwdth`, `polar`, the T/R 25-08 threshold used (band, 25 kHz reference, 50 %/10 %, 10 m), the P.1546 parameters (path type land/sea, time %, h1 from `hgt_agl`/effective heights, receiver height), every assumption (missing heights/gains, ML zone geometry), and citations (Preface page, T/R 25-08 annex, P.1546 section). Include `fxm_rmks.rmk_text` and `fxm_coord` status so reviewers see what the notifier declared about coordination.
6. **Trends/anomalies** are cheap from the same table: counts per issue by `adm` × band × `stn_cls` × `bdwdth_cde` × `nat_srv` (e.g. `IM` = IMT), flagged against the previous issues; the Article 11 index PDFs for many past issues (e.g. 3074–3083) can be parsed without Access to extend the time series back cheaply.
7. **Verification steps the data researcher should run today:** `mdb-tables`, row counts per table, distinct `pub_no`/`pub_prefix`/`pub_part`/`intent`/`fragment`/`notice_typ`/`stn_type`/`geo_type` values, unit of `freq_assgn`/`bdwdth`, whether Plan (GE06L/GE84) notices are present, whether `assgn_id` persists from Part 1 (3074/3075) to Part 2 (3076) for the same notice, and whether MODIFY/SUPPRESS rows carry a usable `target`.
8. **Don't scrape eMIFR/TerRaSys**; they are login-gated. Offer an "optional MIFR layer" that accepts a TIES export.
